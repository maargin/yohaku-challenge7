        How it works

        Local decisions, a shared safety layer, human oversight

        1 · Sense locally

        Each satellite's onboard AI watches its most dangerous neighbour: time to closest approach, miss distance, uncertainty, fuel.

        2 · Announce

        Both sides send PROPOSE with their registered class and fuel. Silence counts: a satellite that never answers is treated as unable to move.

        3 · Same data, same answer

        Both AIs compute the same priority from shared data: debris never moves, crewed holds, low fuel is protected, then fair tie-breaks.

        4 · Safety layer, human, ledger

        A safety layer checks every AI action. High stakes go to a human. The ledger credits whoever yields and charges free-riders.

        Governance

        Who is in charge, who is responsible, and what does everyone owe?

        Responsibility

        When an AI-shaped decision goes wrong, who answers?

        Bad tracking data — the data provider first, the operator second.
        Flawed AI policy — the AI developer, within its declared operating domain.
        Ignored or silent handshake — that operator, presumed responsible.
        False class declaration — the declaring operator; the licensing state supervises.
        Human override that causes harm — the operator whose person decided; every decision is logged with its actor.
        Evidence of fault — whether the safety layer was respected, and what the ledger and decision log show.
        Learning governance

        The network learns. People decide what it learns.

        Neither the safety layer nor the model changes by itself. Like aviation incident boards, the board reviews cases and approves changes, and every version stays on record.

        NARETU obligations charter

        What each actor owes, to whom, and for how long

        Operators oweShared data, a timely handshake, their fair share of dodges, and disposal at end of life.
        AI developers oweExplainable decisions, a bounded operating domain, and triggers they cannot switch off.
        Data providers oweNeutral, timely warnings with honest uncertainty.
        States oweSupervision of the operators they license, and verified registration.
        Everyone owes those not yet launchedA usable orbit: no net addition to long-lived debris, and protection for the small, the stuck and the crewed first.

        NARETU framing from the challenge brief, credited to Chief Titus Letaapo and Samburu obligations-based governance.
