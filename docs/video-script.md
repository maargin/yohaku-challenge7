# 2-minute video script

Total target: **1:55**, leaving 5 seconds of slack. Record the screen at 1440×900 in dark mode, speed "Normal". Words to say are in quotes; shot descriptions are in italics.

| Time | Shot (screen) | Voice-over |
|---|---|---|
| 0:00–0:12 | *Hero section; slow scroll over the two statistics.* | "In six months, Starlink reported more than two hundred thousand collision-avoidance manoeuvres. And in 2019, two satellites negotiated a near-miss by email — and one side never saw the last message." |
| 0:12–0:27 | *Scroll to the 2019 replay; press "Play replay"; let both columns fill.* | "Orbit has no traffic control. Here is the same 2019 encounter twice: on the left what happened — one side dodged alone, late. On the right, both satellites announce themselves, compute the same verdict, and one burns early and small." |
| 0:27–0:40 | *Black title card or plain slide: "What can we learn from mycelium?"* | "The challenge asked what fungal networks can teach us. Fungi coordinate with no boss: each tip reacts locally, the network stays connected, and partners that give back are rewarded. That part holds — so every satellite in our system carries the same small AI and decides locally." |
| 0:40–0:50 | *Same card, second line: "Where the analogy breaks".* | "But the analogy breaks. Fungi can't be held responsible, don't care about fairness, and repair themselves. Orbit can't repair itself. So we borrowed the architecture — not the morality — and added what nature lacks." |
| 0:50–1:12 | *Mission Control, crewed-vs-commercial scenario. Open the AI Brain tab, press Play → escalation dialog opens → hover the three buttons → click Approve → handshake messages appear.* | "Each satellite's onboard AI proposes what to do — you can see its confidence for every action. A safety layer checks every proposal: debris can't move, crewed vehicles hold course, low-fuel satellites are protected. A crewed vehicle is involved, so the system stops and asks a human. The AI can't switch that trigger off. I approve, and the handshake completes." |
| 1:12–1:24 | *Click the Ledger tab; then switch to the silent-counterpart or debris scenario and show the ledger.* | "Every dodge is recorded in a shared ledger. Whoever yields earns credit. Debris owners pay for their debris, and a satellite that stays silent is treated as unable to move — and pays for it." |
| 1:24–1:38 | *Scroll to Live; pick "Cubesat almost out of fuel"; press "Run the AI"; then scroll to Results and hold on the metrics table.* | "The same network runs live in the browser: change the fuel or the tracking uncertainty and watch it decide. We trained it on eight hundred million simulated steps using ESA's conjunction data. On ten thousand new scenarios it ended ninety-nine point three percent safely and resolved nine in ten encounters that would otherwise have collided — always inside the safety layer." |
| 1:38–1:55 | *Scroll to Governance, then the NARETU charter; end on the "those not yet launched" card.* | "Humans stay accountable: every failure has an owner, and neither the safety layer nor the model changes without an independent board. And following NARETU, everyone owes those not yet launched a usable orbit — protecting the small, the stuck and the crewed first." |

## Recording tips
- Close other windows and hide the browser bookmarks bar. Zoom the page to 100%.
- Run through once without recording, so the fonts and data are cached.
- If you stop the recording at the escalation dialog, reload the page to start the scenario again.
- Keep every collision probability on screen labelled "illustrative". They already are.
